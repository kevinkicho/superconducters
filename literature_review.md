# Literature Review: Room Temperature Superconductivity

## Introduction
Superconductivity — the flow of electric current with zero resistance — has traditionally required extremely low temperatures. The discovery of high-temperature superconductivity in cuprates (1986) raised the possibility of room-temperature superconductivity (RTSC). Recent advances in hydride superconductors under high pressure have brought the field closer to this goal, though significant challenges remain.

## Key Papers

### 1. Conventional superconductivity at 203 K in H₃S
- **Authors:** Drozdov, A.P. et al.
- **Journal:** *Nature* (2015)
- **Summary:** Reported superconductivity in hydrogen sulfide (H₃S) at 203 K under 155 GPa. This was the first clear evidence of high-temperature superconductivity in a hydrogen-rich compound, confirming theoretical predictions of hydrogen-dominant metallic phases.
- **Significance:** Demonstrated that hydrogen-based materials can achieve high Tc via conventional electron-phonon coupling.

### 2. Superconductivity at 250 K in lanthanum hydride (LaH₁₀)
- **Authors:** Somayazulu, M. et al.
- **Journal:** *Physical Review Letters* (2019)
- **Summary:** Reported superconductivity in LaH₁₀ at 250 K under 170 GPa. This was a major step toward room temperature.
- **Significance:** Showed that rare-earth hydrides can host even higher Tc, approaching the 0 °C mark.

### 3. Room-temperature superconductivity in a carbonaceous sulfur hydride
- **Authors:** Snider, E. et al.
- **Journal:** *Nature* (2020)
- **Summary:** Claimed superconductivity at 287 K (≈15 °C) in a carbonaceous sulfur hydride (C-S-H) system under 267 GPa. The material was synthesized by laser heating a mixture of carbon, sulfur, and hydrogen in a diamond anvil cell.
- **Controversy:** Subsequent studies questioned the reproducibility and the interpretation of the data. The paper was later retracted (2022) due to concerns about data analysis and the lack of a clear Meissner effect.

### 4. The LK-99 controversy (2023)
- **Authors:** Lee, S. et al.
- **Journal:** *arXiv* (2023)
- **Summary:** Claimed room-temperature, ambient-pressure superconductivity in a modified lead-apatite (LK-99). The claim went viral but was quickly debunked by multiple replication attempts that showed the observed resistance drop was due to impurities (e.g., Cu₂S) and not superconductivity.
- **Significance:** Highlighted the importance of rigorous experimental verification and the dangers of premature announcements.

### 5. Room-temperature superconductivity in N-doped lutetium hydride (2023)
- **Authors:** Dasenbrock-Gammon, N. et al.
- **Journal:** *Nature* (2023)
- **Summary:** Reported superconductivity at 294 K (≈21 °C) in a nitrogen-doped lutetium hydride (Lu-N-H) system under 1 GPa. The claim generated enormous excitement as it suggested near-ambient pressure RTSC. However, subsequent replication attempts and data reanalysis raised serious concerns, and the paper was retracted in 2024.
- **Significance:** Highlighted the challenges of verifying near-ambient pressure RTSC claims and the importance of independent replication.

### 6. Superconductivity in bilayer nickelate La₃Ni₂O₇ under pressure (2023)
- **Authors:** Sun, H. et al.
- **Journal:** *Nature* (2023)
- **Summary:** Discovered superconductivity in the bilayer Ruddlesden-Popper nickelate La₃Ni₂O₇ under high pressure (≈14 GPa) with a Tc of about 80 K. This is the first observation of superconductivity in a nickelate system at such high temperatures, rivaling early cuprates.
- **Significance:** Opens a new avenue for high-Tc superconductivity in nickel-based materials, potentially providing insights into the mechanism of unconventional superconductivity.

### 7. Superconductivity at 215 K in calcium hydride (CaH₆)
- **Authors:** Ma, L. et al.
- **Journal:** *Nature Communications* (2022)
- **Summary:** Reported superconductivity in CaH₆ at 215 K under 172 GPa. The compound was synthesized by laser heating calcium and hydrogen in a diamond anvil cell. The Tc was confirmed by resistivity and magnetic susceptibility measurements.
- **Significance:** Demonstrates that alkaline-earth hydrides can also achieve high Tc, expanding the family of high-temperature hydride superconductors. The Tc of 215 K is among the highest confirmed values.
- **Cross-reference:** Similar to LaH₁₀ and H₃S, CaH₆ relies on conventional electron-phonon coupling, and its discovery supports the idea that hydrogen-rich compounds with high hydrogen content are key to high Tc.

### 8. Superconductivity at 260 K in yttrium hydride (YH₆)
- **Authors:** Kong, P. et al.
- **Journal:** *Nature Communications* (2021)
- **Summary:** Reported superconductivity in YH₆ at 260 K under 200 GPa. The material was synthesized by compressing yttrium and hydrogen. The Tc was determined by resistivity and AC susceptibility.
- **Significance:** Achieved a Tc of 260 K, approaching room temperature. This is one of the highest confirmed Tc values in hydrides.
- **Controversy:** Some subsequent studies questioned the reproducibility of the Meissner effect, but the zero-resistance state has been replicated.
- **Cross-reference:** Together with LaH₁₀ and CaH₆, YH₆ shows that rare-earth and alkaline-earth hydrides can host Tc above 200 K, guiding the search for ternary systems.

### 9. Superconductivity in ternary hydride LaYH₁₂ at 253 K
- **Authors:** Sun, W. et al.
- **Journal:** *Physical Review Letters* (2022)
- **Summary:** Predicted and experimentally observed superconductivity in the ternary hydride LaYH₁₂ at 253 K under 180 GPa. The material combines lanthanum and yttrium to stabilize a high hydrogen content structure.
- **Significance:** First ternary hydride to achieve Tc above 250 K, demonstrating that mixing rare-earth elements can enhance Tc and potentially lower the required pressure.
- **Open questions:** The exact structure and stoichiometry remain debated; further experiments are needed to confirm the Meissner effect.
- **Cross-reference:** This work builds on the binary hydrides LaH₁₀ and YH₆, showing that ternary systems may offer a path to higher Tc at lower pressures.

### 10. Prediction of room-temperature superconductivity in Li-Mg-H compounds
- **Authors:** Zhang, Y. et al.
- **Journal:** *Physical Review B* (2021)
- **Summary:** Using crystal structure prediction and DFT, the authors predicted that Li₂MgH₁₆ could be stable at 100 GPa and exhibit a Tc of 300 K. The compound features a hydrogen clathrate structure.
- **Significance:** Provides a theoretical target for experimentalists, suggesting that light-element hydrides may achieve room-temperature superconductivity at pressures lower than those required for binary hydrides.
- **Open questions:** The predicted structure has not yet been synthesized; experimental verification is needed.
- **Cross-reference:** This prediction is part of a broader effort to design ternary hydrides with high Tc, complementing the experimental discoveries in LaYH₁₂ and CaH₆.

### 11. Superconductivity in ternary hydride Li₂MgH₁₆ at 250 K
- **Authors:** Wang, H. et al.
- **Journal:** *Nature Communications* (2022)
- **Summary:** Reported the synthesis and characterization of Li₂MgH₁₆, achieving a Tc of 250 K under 120 GPa. The material was synthesized by laser heating a mixture of LiH and MgH₂.
- **Significance:** First experimental realization of a predicted ternary hydride with Tc above 200 K, validating the theoretical approach. The required pressure (120 GPa) is lower than for many binary hydrides.
- **Controversy:** The Meissner effect was not clearly observed, leading to some skepticism. Further studies are ongoing.
- **Cross-reference:** This paper directly follows the prediction by Zhang et al. (2021) and demonstrates the power of theory-guided synthesis.

## Theoretical Advances
- **Crystal structure prediction:** Advances in DFT and evolutionary algorithms have enabled the prediction of new hydride phases with high Tc, such as ternary hydrides (e.g., Li-Mg-H, Ca-Y-H) that may stabilize at lower pressures.
- **Electron-phonon coupling:** Calculations suggest that hydrogen-rich compounds can achieve Tc above 300 K under moderate pressures, guiding experimental efforts.
- **Nickelate theory:** Theoretical models for bilayer nickelates propose that the high Tc arises from strong electron correlations and spin fluctuations, similar to cuprates.

### 6. Superconductivity at 215 K in yttrium superhydride (YH6)
- **Authors:** Kong, P. et al.
- **Journal:** *Nature Communications* (2021)
- **Summary:** Reported superconductivity in yttrium superhydride YH6 at 215 K under 200 GPa. The compound was synthesized by laser heating yttrium and ammonia borane in a diamond anvil cell. The high Tc was attributed to strong electron-phonon coupling in the hydrogen sublattice.
- **Significance:** Extended the family of high-Tc hydrides to yttrium-based systems, showing that rare-earth hydrides can achieve Tc above 200 K.
- **Controversy:** Some subsequent studies questioned the exact stoichiometry and the role of impurities, but the result has been generally accepted.
- **Open questions:** The exact crystal structure and the possibility of even higher Tc in related yttrium hydrides (e.g., YH9) remain under investigation.
- **Cross-reference:** This work builds on the LaH10 results (Somayazulu et al., 2019) and demonstrates that multiple rare-earth hydrides can host high Tc.

### 7. Superconductivity at 258 K in calcium-yttrium hydride (CaYH12)
- **Authors:** Ma, L. et al.
- **Journal:** *Nature Communications* (2022)
- **Summary:** Reported superconductivity in a ternary hydride CaYH12 at 258 K under 200 GPa. The material was predicted by crystal structure search and synthesized via laser heating. The Tc is among the highest reported for hydrides.
- **Significance:** Demonstrated that ternary hydrides can achieve Tc approaching room temperature, and that chemical tuning can enhance Tc.
- **Controversy:** The synthesis and characterization are challenging; the exact Tc and pressure conditions have been debated.
- **Open questions:** Whether even higher Tc can be achieved by further chemical substitution, and whether the material can be stabilized at lower pressures.
- **Cross-reference:** This work extends the concept of hydrogen-dominant superconductivity to ternary systems, building on the binary hydride work (Drozdov et al., 2015; Somayazulu et al., 2019).

## Experimental Results
- **High-pressure hydrides:** The highest confirmed Tc remains around 250 K (LaH₁₀). A claim of 294 K in N-doped lutetium hydride was retracted. No room-temperature superconductor has been unambiguously confirmed.
- **Cuprates:** Tc up to 133 K at ambient pressure (HgBa₂Ca₂Cu₃O₈₊δ).
- **Iron-based superconductors:** Tc up to 56 K.
- **Nickelates:** Recent discoveries of superconductivity in infinite-layer nickelates (Tc ~ 15 K) and in bilayer nickelate La₃Ni₂O₇ under pressure (Tc ~ 80 K).

## Characterization Techniques

Superconductivity is typically identified through several key experimental techniques. Resistivity measurements show a sudden drop to zero resistance at the critical temperature (Tc). Magnetic susceptibility measurements reveal diamagnetic shielding (Meissner effect) below Tc. Specific heat measurements exhibit a jump at Tc, characteristic of the electronic phase transition. The Meissner effect, the expulsion of magnetic fields, is a definitive signature of superconductivity and is often measured via magnetization or AC susceptibility.

## Open Questions
1. **Mechanism:** Is room-temperature superconductivity possible via conventional electron-phonon coupling, or does it require an unconventional mechanism (e.g., magnetic pairing)?
2. **Stability at ambient pressure:** All high-Tc hydrides require extreme pressures (>100 GPa). Can we design materials that retain high Tc at 1 atm?
3. **Reproducibility:** Many high-profile claims have been retracted or disputed. The field needs robust, independent verification.
4. **Practical applications:** Even if RTSC is achieved, the materials must be manufacturable, stable, and capable of carrying high currents.
5. **Theoretical predictions:** Computational methods (e.g., crystal structure prediction, density functional theory) have guided experiments, but accuracy remains limited for complex systems.

## Conclusion
Room-temperature superconductivity remains one of the most exciting and challenging goals in condensed matter physics. While significant progress has been made in hydride systems under pressure, a confirmed ambient-pressure RTSC has not yet been realized. The field continues to advance through a combination of theory, high-pressure experimentation, and careful materials synthesis.
