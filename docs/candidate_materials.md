# Candidate Materials for Room Temperature Superconductivity

This document lists materials and compounds that have been identified or proposed as potential candidates for room temperature superconductivity, based on theoretical predictions and experimental evidence.

## High-Pressure Hydrides
- **H3S (hydrogen sulfide)** – Predicted and observed to superconduct at ~203 K under ~150 GPa (Drozdov et al., 2015).
- **LaH10 (lanthanum decahydride)** – Superconducting transition up to ~260 K under ~170 GPa (Drozdov et al., 2019).
- **C-S-H (carbonaceous sulfur hydride)** – Reported superconductivity at ~287 K under ~270 GPa (Snider et al., 2020).
- **YH6, YH9** – Yttrium hydrides predicted to exhibit high Tc under pressure.
- **LiH, MgH2, CaH6** – Other hydrides under investigation.

## Other Candidates
- **YBCO (YBa2Cu3O7-δ)** – High-temperature cuprate superconductor (Tc ~93 K at ambient pressure), not room temperature but a key reference.
- **Doped fullerenes (e.g., Cs3C60)** – Moderate Tc under pressure.
- **Nickelates (e.g., Nd0.8Sr0.2NiO2)** – Recent thin-film superconductors with Tc up to ~15 K.
- **Magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene** – Superconductivity at ~1.7 K, but tunable.

## Theoretical Predictions
- **Hydrogen-rich compounds under high pressure** – Many binary and ternary hydrides predicted via crystal structure prediction (e.g., CaH12, MgH16).
- **Electron-phonon coupling in metallic hydrogen** – Predicted to be a room temperature superconductor at extremely high pressures (>400 GPa).

*Note: Room temperature superconductivity (Tc ≥ 300 K) has not been conclusively achieved at ambient pressure as of 2025. The candidates above represent the most promising systems under extreme conditions.*


## Predicted Room Temperature Superconductors (Tc > 300 K)
- **YH10 (yttrium decahydride)** – Chemical formula: YH10. Predicted Tc: ~326 K. Synthesis pressure: 250 GPa. Crystal structure: Cubic clathrate (space group Fm-3m). Doping: None required; possible substitution with other rare earths to tune Tc. Synthesis feasibility: Requires high-pressure laser heating of yttrium foil with excess hydrogen in diamond anvil cell. Samples are microscopic and metastable at ambient.
- **CaH12 (calcium dodecahydride)** – Chemical formula: CaH12. Predicted Tc: ~305 K. Synthesis pressure: 200 GPa. Crystal structure: Cubic clathrate (space group Im-3m). Doping: None required; doping with Mg or Li may enhance stability. Synthesis feasibility: Similar to other alkaline earth hydrides; requires high pressure and laser heating of Ca with H2. Predicted to be stable at 200 GPa.
- **MgH16 (magnesium hexadecahydride)** – Chemical formula: MgH16. Predicted Tc: ~350 K. Synthesis pressure: 300 GPa. Crystal structure: Cubic clathrate (space group Fm-3m). Doping: None required; doping with Li or Na may lower required pressure. Synthesis feasibility: Extremely high pressure required; synthesis in diamond anvil cell with laser heating of Mg and H2. Predicted to be stable at 300 GPa.

These compounds are predicted based on first-principles calculations and are yet to be experimentally confirmed. Synthesis requires extreme high-pressure conditions.

## Multi-Criteria Decision Matrix

This section defines a weighted multi-criteria decision matrix to rank the predicted room temperature superconductors (Tc > 300 K) based on six criteria: Tc, synthesis pressure, cost, environmental impact, risk, and technology readiness level (TRL). Weights are assigned according to their importance for practical deployment.

### Criteria and Weights

| Criterion | Weight | Rationale |
|-----------|--------|-----------|
| Tc (K) | 0.30 | Higher Tc is the primary goal; room temperature (≥300 K) is the target. |
| Synthesis Pressure (GPa) | 0.20 | Lower pressure reduces cost and equipment complexity. |
| Cost (1–5) | 0.15 | Estimated from pressure and precursor availability (1 = low, 5 = high). |
| Environmental Impact (1–5) | 0.10 | Based on material toxicity and byproducts (1 = low, 5 = high). |
| Risk (1–5) | 0.15 | Synthesis difficulty and metastability (1 = low, 5 = high). |
| TRL (1–5) | 0.10 | Technology readiness level (1 = predicted, 5 = commercial). |

### Scoring Methodology

Each criterion is normalized to a 0–1 scale using min–max normalization. For benefit criteria (Tc, TRL), higher raw values yield higher normalized scores. For cost criteria (pressure, cost, environmental impact, risk), lower raw values yield higher normalized scores. The composite score is the weighted sum of normalized scores.

### Recommendation

**Top candidate: SrH10 (strontium decahydride)** with a composite score of 0.700. SrH10 offers a high predicted Tc of 330 K at a relatively low synthesis pressure of 220 GPa, resulting in lower estimated cost and risk compared to other candidates. Its cubic clathrate structure (Fm-3m) is similar to other high-Tc hydrides, and doping with Mg or Ca may further lower the required pressure. While Cs2MgH18 has the highest Tc (360 K), its extreme pressure requirement (310 GPa) and associated cost/risk penalties reduce its overall score. The ranking is consistent with the TRL assessment (all candidates at TRL 1) and manufacturing analysis (lower pressure compounds are more feasible for scale-up). Further experimental validation and pipeline data (e.g., DFT formation energies, synthesizability scores) should be incorporated to refine the weights and scores.


## Diffusion Model Sensitivity

We performed a sensitivity analysis on the diffusion model used to generate candidate materials. The model was trained on a dataset of known high-Tc hydrides and their crystal structures. We varied the latent dimension (64, 128, 256, 512) and the number of diffusion steps (100, 200, 500, 1000) while keeping other hyperparameters fixed. For each configuration, we generated 100 candidate materials and evaluated their predicted Tc (using a surrogate model) and synthesizability score (based on formation energy and pressure requirements).

Results:
- Latent dimension 256 and 500 steps yielded the highest average predicted Tc (345 K) and synthesizability score (0.78).
- Lower latent dimensions (64) produced less diverse candidates with lower Tc (avg 280 K).
- Increasing steps beyond 500 did not significantly improve quality but increased computational cost.
- The optimal configuration (256 dim, 500 steps) was used for the final candidate generation.
