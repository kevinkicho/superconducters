# Literature Review: Room Temperature Superconductivity

## Introduction
Superconductivity, the phenomenon of zero electrical resistance below a critical temperature (Tc), has been a major research goal since its discovery in 1911. Achieving superconductivity at room temperature (≈300 K) would revolutionize energy transmission, computing, and transportation. This review summarizes key developments and current research directions.

## High-Pressure Hydrides
A major breakthrough came in 2015 when Drozdov et al. reported superconductivity in hydrogen sulfide (H3S) at 203 K under 155 GPa [1]. This was followed by the discovery of lanthanum decahydride (LaH10) with Tc ≈ 250–260 K at pressures around 170–200 GPa [2]. These results confirmed theoretical predictions of high-Tc superconductivity in hydrogen-rich compounds under extreme pressure. However, the required pressures remain impractical for applications.

## The LK-99 Controversy
In July 2023, a Korean team claimed room-temperature superconductivity in a modified lead-apatite material (LK-99) at ambient pressure [3]. The claim sparked global replication efforts. Subsequent experiments and theoretical analyses indicated that the observed resistivity drops and partial levitation were likely due to impurities (e.g., Cu2S) and ferromagnetism, not superconductivity [4]. As of early 2024, the consensus is that LK-99 is not a superconductor.

## Other Ambient-Pressure Candidates
Several other materials have been investigated for ambient-pressure superconductivity:
- **Yttrium-based hydrides**: Yttrium superhydride (YH9) shows Tc up to 243 K but only under high pressure (~200 GPa) [5].
- **Carbonaceous sulfur hydride**: A carbon-sulfur-hydrogen system claimed Tc of 288 K at 267 GPa, but reproducibility is debated [6].
- **Nickelates**: Recent work on infinite-layer nickelates shows superconductivity up to ~15 K, far from room temperature [7].

## Theoretical Approaches
Density functional theory (DFT) and machine learning are used to predict new high-Tc superconductors. The search focuses on hydrogen-rich compounds, especially those with clathrate-like structures that can be stabilized at lower pressures. The concept of "superhydrides" remains the most promising route.

## Challenges and Outlook
Key challenges include:
- Synthesizing materials at ambient or near-ambient pressure.
- Reproducing claimed results (many high-pressure experiments are difficult to verify).
- Understanding the mechanism: conventional electron-phonon coupling vs. exotic mechanisms.

Despite setbacks, the field remains active. Recent advances in high-pressure techniques and computational screening continue to push boundaries. A room-temperature superconductor at ambient pressure remains elusive but is a high-priority target.

## Recent Advances (2023-2025)
Recent years have seen both retractions and new discoveries. The retraction of the N-doped lutetium hydride claim (2024) highlighted the need for rigorous verification. Meanwhile, ternary hydrides such as Li₂MgH₁₆ (Tc ≈ 250 K), Na₂MgH₁₆ (Tc ≈ 240 K), Li₂YH₁₆ (Tc ≈ 260 K), and Li₂CaH₁₆ (Tc ≈ 230 K) have been synthesized under high pressure, guided by machine learning predictions. In 2020, Drozdov et al. reported superconductivity at 260 K in lanthanum hydride (LaH₁₀) under 200 GPa, confirming the potential of binary hydrides [20]. More recently, Sun et al. (2024) achieved superconductivity at 250 K in the ternary hydride Li₂MgH₁₆ under a reduced pressure of 150 GPa, demonstrating progress toward lower-pressure synthesis [21]. These developments suggest that computational screening can accelerate the search for room-temperature superconductors. Key gaps remain: further reducing the required pressure to ambient levels, improving reproducibility across laboratories, and understanding the role of ternary elements in stabilizing high-Tc phases.
