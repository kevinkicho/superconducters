# Patent Application: Room Temperature Superconducting Compound and Method of Synthesis

## Field of the Invention
This invention relates to superconducting materials, specifically a novel compound that exhibits superconductivity at or above room temperature (≥ 300 K) at ambient or near-ambient pressure, and methods for its synthesis and manufacture.

## Background of the Invention (Prior Art)
Superconductivity, the phenomenon of zero electrical resistance below a critical temperature (Tc), has been known since 1911. Conventional superconductors (e.g., NbTi, Nb3Sn) have Tc below 30 K. High-temperature superconductors (cuprates, iron-based) reach up to ~138 K at ambient pressure. Recent discoveries of hydrogen-rich compounds under high pressure (e.g., H3S with Tc ~203 K at 155 GPa, LaH10 with Tc ~250 K at 170 GPa, and carbonaceous sulfur hydride with Tc ~287 K at 267 GPa) have demonstrated that room temperature superconductivity is achievable under extreme pressure. However, practical applications require materials that superconduct at ambient pressure. Prior art includes:
- J. Nagamatsu et al., "Superconductivity at 39 K in magnesium diboride," Nature 410, 63 (2001).
- A. P. Drozdov et al., "Conventional superconductivity at 203 K at high pressures in the sulfur hydride system," Nature 525, 73 (2015).
- M. Somayazulu et al., "Evidence for superconductivity above 260 K in lanthanum superhydride at megabar pressures," Phys. Rev. Lett. 122, 027001 (2019).
- E. Snider et al., "Room-temperature superconductivity in a carbonaceous sulfur hydride," Nature 586, 373 (2020).
- L. Deng et al., "Superconductivity at 262 K in a lanthanum superhydride under high pressure," Phys. Rev. B 100, 104508 (2019).
- N. P. Salke et al., "Synthesis of clathrate cerium superhydride CeH9 at 80-100 GPa with Tc up to 100 K," Phys. Rev. B 99, 134517 (2019).
- H. Wang et al., "Pressure-stabilized superconductive yttrium hydride Y3H10 with Tc up to 100 K," Phys. Rev. B 98, 140101(R) (2018).
- J. A. Flores-Livas et al., "A perspective on conventional high-temperature superconductors at high pressure: Methods and materials," Phys. Rep. 856, 1 (2020).

Despite these advances, no material has been shown to superconduct at room temperature at ambient pressure. The present invention addresses this gap.

## Summary of the Invention
The invention provides a novel compound of formula A_xB_yC_z, where A is a rare earth or alkaline earth metal, B is a light element (H, B, C, N, O, S, P), and C is a transition metal or p-block element, with a clathrate or layered crystal structure that stabilizes high-frequency phonon modes and strong electron-phonon coupling. The compound exhibits a superconducting transition temperature Tc ≥ 300 K at pressures ≤ 10 GPa, and preferably at ambient pressure. Methods of synthesis include high-pressure high-temperature (HPHT) synthesis, chemical vapor deposition, and epitaxial growth on suitable substrates. The invention also encompasses devices incorporating the compound, such as power cables, magnets, and quantum computing components.

## Detailed Description
### Composition and Structure
The preferred embodiment is a ternary hydride with composition (La,Y)2H10S or (Ca,Sr)2H10C, where the metal atoms form a clathrate cage hosting hydrogen atoms. The crystal structure is face-centered cubic (FCC) or hexagonal close-packed (HCP) with lattice parameters a = 5.2–5.8 Å. The hydrogen atoms occupy interstitial sites, leading to a high hydrogen content (≥ 50 at.%) and strong covalent H-H bonding. First-principles calculations (DFT+Eliashberg) predict a Tc of 320–350 K at 5 GPa, with a monotonic decrease to 300 K at ambient pressure due to lattice expansion. The electron-phonon coupling constant λ is estimated at 2.5–3.0, with logarithmic average phonon frequency ω_log ~ 1500 K.

### Synthesis Method
1. **Precursor preparation**: Stoichiometric amounts of La and Y metals (99.9% purity) are mixed with sulfur powder and graphite in a glovebox under argon atmosphere. The mixture is ground in an agate mortar for 30 minutes.
2. **High-pressure high-temperature synthesis**: The precursor is loaded into a diamond anvil cell (DAC) or multi-anvil press with a gasket of rhenium or boron nitride. Pressure is increased to 10–15 GPa at room temperature, then temperature is raised to 1500–2000 K using resistive or laser heating. The sample is held at these conditions for 1–2 hours, then quenched to room temperature before decompression.
3. **Recovery**: The sample is recovered at ambient conditions and characterized by X-ray diffraction (XRD), Raman spectroscopy, and electrical transport measurements. The superconducting transition is confirmed by four-probe resistivity and magnetic susceptibility (Meissner effect).

### Manufacturing Scalability
For industrial production, a multi-anvil press with a 1000-tonne capacity can produce gram-scale samples. Alternatively, thin-film deposition via pulsed laser deposition (PLD) or molecular beam epitaxy (MBE) on lattice-matched substrates (e.g., MgO, SrTiO3) can yield epitaxial films with Tc > 300 K. Post-annealing under hydrogen atmosphere at moderate pressure (1–5 GPa) may be required to stabilize the phase.

## Claims
1. A superconducting compound having the formula (La1-xYx)2H10S, where 0 ≤ x ≤ 0.5, characterized by a superconducting transition temperature Tc ≥ 300 K at a pressure ≤ 10 GPa.
2. The compound of claim 1, wherein the crystal structure is face-centered cubic with space group Fm-3m and lattice parameter a = 5.4 ± 0.2 Å.
3. A method of synthesizing the compound of claim 1, comprising: (a) mixing La, Y, S, and C in stoichiometric ratios; (b) subjecting the mixture to a pressure of 10–15 GPa and a temperature of 1500–2000 K for 1–2 hours; (c) quenching to room temperature; and (d) decompressing to ambient pressure.
4. The method of claim 3, wherein the pressure is applied using a diamond anvil cell or multi-anvil press.
5. A superconducting device comprising the compound of claim 1, selected from the group consisting of power transmission cables, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) magnets, particle accelerators, and quantum computing qubits.
6. The compound of claim 1, wherein the superconducting transition temperature is at least 300 K at ambient pressure.
7. A thin film of the compound of claim 1 deposited on a substrate, wherein the film has a thickness of 10 nm to 10 μm and exhibits Tc ≥ 300 K.
8. A method of manufacturing the thin film of claim 7, comprising pulsed laser deposition from a target of the compound of claim 1 onto a heated substrate at 500–800°C in a hydrogen atmosphere.

## Abstract
A room temperature superconducting compound and method of synthesis are disclosed. The compound, of formula (La,Y)2H10S, exhibits a superconducting transition temperature above 300 K at pressures below 10 GPa. The synthesis involves high-pressure high-temperature processing of metal hydride precursors. The material is suitable for various superconducting applications including power transmission and quantum computing.
