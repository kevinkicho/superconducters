# Experimental Protocol: Carbon-Based Superconductors

## Objective
Synthesize and characterize carbon-based superconducting compounds (e.g., alkali-doped C₆₀, graphite intercalation compounds, or boron-doped diamond) targeting room-temperature superconductivity.

## Materials
- C₆₀ powder (99.9% purity, sublimed)
- Alkali metals: K, Rb, Cs (99.9%, stored under mineral oil)
- Highly oriented pyrolytic graphite (HOPG) or single-crystal graphite
- Boron (99.99%) for diamond doping
- High-pressure apparatus (e.g., multi-anvil press, diamond anvil cell)
- Argon-filled glovebox (O₂, H₂O < 0.1 ppm)
- Quartz ampoules, tantalum foil, stainless steel capsules

## Synthesis Steps
1. **Alkali-doped C₆₀ (A₃C₆₀)**: In glovebox, weigh C₆₀ and alkali metal (e.g., K) in 1:3 molar ratio. Seal in quartz ampoule under 200 mbar Ar. Heat at 400°C for 24 h, then slow cool to room temperature. Product is black powder.
2. **Graphite intercalation compound (GIC)**: Use two-zone vapor transport. Place graphite flake and alkali metal (e.g., Rb) in separate zones of evacuated quartz tube. Heat metal zone to 200°C, graphite zone to 300°C for 48 h. Stage-1 compound forms.
3. **Boron-doped diamond (BDD)**: High-pressure high-temperature (HPHT) synthesis. Mix diamond powder (1–2 μm) with boron (0.1–1 at.%) in a ball mill. Load into Ta capsule, press at 5–6 GPa and 1400–1600°C for 30 min using multi-anvil press. Quench to room temperature.
4. **Carbon nanotube (CNT) doping**: Arc-discharge synthesis with boron or nitrogen doping. Use graphite rods with B₂O₃ or melamine in He atmosphere. Collect soot and purify by acid treatment.
5. **Post-treatment**: For C₆₀ compounds, anneal at 200°C under dynamic vacuum to remove excess alkali. For GICs, protect from air exposure by coating with epoxy or storing in glovebox.

## Characterization Methods
- **X-ray diffraction (XRD)**: Identify phase (e.g., fcc A₃C₆₀, stage index for GICs, diamond cubic for BDD).
- **Raman spectroscopy**: Characterize C₆₀ phonon modes (A_g(2) shift upon doping), G-band for graphite/diamond.
- **Resistivity**: Four-probe or van der Pauw method from 2–300 K; measure T_c and normal-state behavior.
- **Magnetization**: SQUID or AC susceptibility to confirm superconductivity (Meissner effect).
- **Scanning electron microscopy (SEM)**: Morphology and grain size.
- **X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS)**: Determine doping level and chemical state (e.g., C 1s, B 1s, K 2p).
- **Thermal analysis (DSC/TGA)**: Check stability and decomposition temperatures.

## Safety Guidelines
- **Alkali metals**: Highly reactive with water/air. Handle only in glovebox with inert atmosphere. Use small quantities; quench waste with isopropanol in fume hood.
- **High-pressure apparatus**: Use certified equipment with blast shields. Follow manufacturer's safety protocols for multi-anvil press.
- **C₆₀ dust**: May be irritant; use fume hood and avoid inhalation.
- **Boron and diamond powder**: Inert but fine dust; use dust mask.
- **Arc-discharge**: UV radiation and high voltage; use welding goggles and insulated gloves.
- **Chemical waste**: Alkali metal residues must be neutralized carefully; carbon materials can be disposed as solid waste after deactivation.
- **Emergency**: For alkali metal burns, cover with dry sand or class D fire extinguisher; do not use water. Seek immediate medical attention.
